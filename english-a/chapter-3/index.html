Q1 What is the central idea of the poem "Daffodils"?

The central idea of the poem "Daffodils" by William Wordsworth is that the beauty of nature can bring immense joy and inspiration. The beautiful memories of nature can cheer one in later times through the power of imagination.

2. Describe the setting where the poet saw the daffodils. How does this setting contribute to the overall mood of the poem?

Ans:The poet saw the daffodils by a lake, under trees, with a gentle breeze. This serene setting creates a peaceful and joyful mood.

3. How does the poet describe the daffodils? What imagery does he use to bring them to life?

Ans: The poet describes the daffodils as "fluttering and dancing" in the breeze, using vivid imagery like "golden" and "swaying" to bring them to life.

4. Identify and explain the use of personification in the poem.

Ans: The poet uses personification by describing the daffodils as "dancing" and "fluttering", giving them human-like qualities, as if they are alive and moving to the breeze.

5. What is the effect of the rhyme scheme on the poem's rhythm and flow?

The rhyme scheme creates a musical quality, adding a lively and playful touch to the poem, which complements the joyful description of the daffodils.

6. Why do you think the poet describes the daffodils as "jocund company"? What impact do they have on him?

Ans: The poet describes the daffodils as "jocund company" because they bring him joy and happiness.

7. The poet mentions the "bliss of solitude." What do you think he means by this, and how do the daffodils contribute to this feeling?

Ans: The poet means a state of happy isolation. The daffodils contribute to this feeling by

providing a sense of peaceful companionship.

8. Imagine you are the poet revisiting the same place after many years. Write a short paragraph describing your feelings and the scene as it might appear now.

Though years have flown, the scene remains so bright, the vibrant hues, a solace to my soul, a timeless dance of light and pure delight, where whispers of the past still make me whole. For in this field, where daffodils still sway, I find a refuge, where my spirit can stay.
