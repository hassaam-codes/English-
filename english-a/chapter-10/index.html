Q1.Why is the predator-prey relationship between the snow leopard and the markhor important for the ecosystem?

Ans: the predator-prey relationship between the snow leopard and the markhor is important for the ecosystem because it maintains the natural balance in the ecosystem.

2. How do the snow leopard and the markhor's adaptations help them survive in the mountains?

Ans: Adaptations like thick coats, wide paws, and long tails help them survive in the mountains.

Q3. What are the benefits and potential concerns of community-led conservation programmes like trophy hunting?

Ans: Benefits:

1. Revenue for conservation

2. Incentives for local protection

Concerns :

1. Sustainability

2. Regulation

Q4. What might happen to the mountain ecosystem if either the snow leopard or the markhor disappeared?

Ans: The ecosystem would be at risk, with potential overgrazing and soil erosion. This could disturb the natural balance of the mountain ecosystem.

Q5. How do human activities like poaching and habitat destruction threaten the balance between the snow leopard and the markhor?

Ans: Poaching and habitat destruction reduce populations and disrupt habitats. These activities can make it difficult for both animals to survive.

Q6. How is the markhor adapted to living in mountainous regions?

Ans: Wide hooves and seasonal coat changes help the markhor adapt. These adaptations help it move easily and survive in harsh mountain conditions.

Q7. What more can be done to raise awareness about protecting endangered species like the snow leopard and the markhor?

Ans: Educate local communities, support conservation, and promote eco-tourism. These efforts can encourage people to protect wildlife and their natural habitats.

Q8. How did the description of the snow leopard and the markhor impact your understanding of these animals and their environment?

Ans: The description highlighted unique adaptations, importance of conservation, and threats from human activities. It helped me understand why these animals are important to the mountain ecosystem.
